Lost Tribes Institute · Founded by Margot Crossing

Tracing the scattered tribes of Israel among the nations

The Lost Tribes Institute is a network of scholars working with indigenous nations and other peoples who say they descend from the tribes of Israel's northern kingdom, exiled in the 8th century BC. Their traditions often carry biblical stories and customs of their own. The Institute organizes round tables and symposiums across Asia and publishes what is presented.

Margot Crossing

Founder, Lost Tribes Institute · Researcher of oral history across Asia

Margot Crossing founded the Lost Tribes Institute to bring together the peoples, scholars and communities who trace their roots to the scattered tribes of Israel. Her own research follows a single thread across Asia: people after people who remember a sacred book they once had and lost.

She lived in Mizoram, northeast India, off and on from 2009 to 2019, listening to the elders and researchers of the Zo peoples, the Mizo, Kuki and Chin. From there her work reaches to the hill tribes of Myanmar, the Karen, the Q’iang of southwest China and the Kyrgyz of Central Asia, linking their epics, chants and customs into one migration story.

She has presented at symposiums in Kyrgyzstan, Mizoram, Meghalaya, Assam, Thailand, Japan, Arizona, Australia and Vanuatu. She has also transcribed and republished the 19th-century accounts of Francis Mason and George Moore on the Karen. Her book From Promise to Restoration is a six-week study of the prophets’ promise that scattered Israel and the nations will be restored.

“We have all heard about the peoples of ‘The Book.’ Even more epic may be the collective memories of the peoples of ‘The Lost Book.’”From The Peoples of the Lost Book, Bishkek, 2019
