*Expanded from a post on X by Rabbi Brian Samuel ([@rabbriansamuel](https://x.com/rabbriansamuel)).*

Judaism frequently argues that there is no scriptural basis for the Messiah coming twice. One Messiah, one arrival, one kingdom set up in plain sight. Since Yeshua did not end war or rule from Jerusalem, the argument goes, he cannot be the one.

But the objection does not come from Tanakh. The Hebrew prophets describe the Messiah's arrival in two ways.

## Two pictures of one King

Zechariah sees the King of Israel arrive "lowly and riding on a donkey, on a colt, the foal of a donkey" (Zechariah 9:9). He comes gentle, bringing salvation, to a city that must be told to rejoice.

Daniel sees "one like the Son of Man, coming with the clouds of heaven." He is brought before the Ancient of Days and given dominion, glory and a kingdom that all peoples, nations and languages will serve forever (Daniel 7:13–14).

Both are the Messiah's arrival, but both cannot depict the same event.

The Talmud saw the tension. In Sanhedrin 98a, Rabbi Alexandri reports Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi setting the two verses side by side: if Israel is worthy, the Messiah comes with the clouds of heaven; if not, lowly and riding on a donkey. ([Jews for Jesus, quoting the Soncino translation](https://jewsforjesus.org/messianic-prophecies/the-messiah-would-come-riding-on-a-donkey)) Orthodox Judaism's Chabad agrees. Their own summary of the passage says: "If the Jews are worthy, Mashiach will come 'with the clouds of the heaven.' If they are not worthy, he will come as 'a poor man riding on a donkey.'" ([Chabad](https://www.chabad.org/therebbe/article_cdo/aid/4807016/jewish/Vayishlach-Why-Mashiach-Will-Ride-a-Donkey.htm))

Notice that the ancient rabbis recognize both verses speak of the Messiah. They kept both, and made one depend on Israel's merit. However you want to slice it, Tanakh reveals two entrances of the Messiah.

## The pattern runs all through Tanakh

Riding the donkey and the clouds are not the only pair. The prophets show a Messiah who suffers and a Messiah who reigns.

- Isaiah's servant is "despised and rejected," "pierced for our transgressions," and "cut off from the land of the living" (Isaiah 53:3–8). The same prophet promises a child on David's throne whose government and peace will have no end (Isaiah 9:6–7).
- Daniel says an Anointed One will be "cut off" before the city and the sanctuary are destroyed (Daniel 9:26). That clock went off in the first century.
- David's Lord is told, "Sit at My right hand until I make Your enemies Your footstool" (Psalm 110:1). There is a waiting period between the seating and the victory.
- God says through Hosea, "I will return again to My place till they acknowledge their offense and seek My face" (Hosea 5:15). He leaves, waits, and then comes back.
- In the last days Israel will "look on Me whom they pierced" and mourn as for an only son (Zechariah 12:10). He is pierced first and recognized later.

Other typologies, like that of Joseph who disappeared as the beloved son and rose again as savior and ruler, are common in Jewish scripture.

The rabbis felt this weight too. Rather than one Messiah coming twice, the Talmud splits him into two men: Messiah son of Joseph, who is killed, and Messiah son of David, who reigns (Sukkah 52a). They found two roles in Scripture and gave each a different body.

Torah itself sets the pattern in the lives of Israel's deliverers. Joseph, as we just mentioned, and Moses, who was pushed away the first time: "Who made you a prince and a judge over us?" (Exodus 2:14). Forty years later he came back and led them out. The deliverer is refused at his first coming and received at his second.

## One Messiah, two comings

Here is the truth: there is ONE Messiah, who came ONCE riding on a donkey, and who WILL COME AGAIN in the clouds of heaven. That is the only way to reconcile the Tanakh without creating a two-messiah heresy.

Yeshua fulfilled the first. He sent for a donkey's colt and rode into Jerusalem while the crowds cried "Hosanna to the Son of David," and Matthew quotes Zechariah word for word (Matthew 21:1–9). Days later, on trial before the high priest, He claimed the second for Himself: "You will see the Son of Man sitting at the right hand of the Power, and coming on the clouds of heaven" (Matthew 26:64). He joined Psalm 110 and Daniel 7 in one sentence, and the high priest tore his robes because he understood exactly what was being claimed.

After He rose and was taken up, two men in white told His followers: "This same Jesus, who was taken up from you into heaven, will come in like manner as you saw Him go" (Acts 1:11). John adds: "Behold, He is coming with clouds, and every eye will see Him, even they who pierced Him" (Revelation 1:7), Zechariah 12:10 and Daniel 7:13 together again.

The rabbis were right that both pictures belong to the Messiah. They were right that Israel's response matters. They only missed the order. The Anointed One came first lowly, to be pierced as the Lamb. He returns on the clouds as King, and this time His own people will know Him, as Joseph's brothers finally knew Joseph.

Yeshua fulfilled the first and now waits until He will fulfill the second. I welcome discussion.
