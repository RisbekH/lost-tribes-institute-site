When the message of Messiah reaches a new people, what happens to the culture they already have? The Western church has given two answers. Pope Gregory the Great reused what was there. Don Richardson recognized what God had already put there. The Lost Tribes Institute is not a Christian organization, but sees the biblical basis for both, while going one step further.

## Pope Gregory the Great: reuse what is there

In 601, Gregory wrote to Abbot Mellitus, who was on his way to join Augustine's mission in England. His instructions were plain: do not destroy the pagan temples. Destroy the idols, sprinkle the buildings with holy water, set up altars, and turn them to the worship of the true God. ([letter](https://romanletters.org/letters/gregory_great/11076))

He did the same with the feasts. The English killed cattle in sacrifice to their gods. Gregory told the missionaries to let the people build shelters of branches around the converted temples and hold feasts there, now eating the cattle with thanks to God. His aim was step by step: people keep their familiar places and national identity while their hearts turn to God.

The Lost Tribes Institute notices that those branch shelters sound a lot like the booths of Sukkot. And the sacrifices appear in the Torah. Gregory's approach has a limit: it reuses pagan forms, but never asks what God had already said to that people. Historians also note Gregory allowed the feasts only in a changed form, and was known as a destroyer of idols. ([Barr](https://bethallisonbarr.substack.com/p/did-medieval-christians-accommodate)) Nonetheless, he didn't raze the culture like outside religions often do as they gain the upper hand.

## Don Richardson: recognize what God already planted

Don Richardson (1935–2018) was a Canadian missionary who went to the Sawi of New Guinea in 1962. ([Field Partner](https://g1.fieldpartner.org/?p=4321)) Telling them about Jesus failed at first: being cannibals, they admired Judas as the hero of the story. Thankfully, God's key was already in their own culture. When warring villages made peace, each tribe gave a child to the other, and peace lasted as long as that child lived. Richardson called this a redemptive analogy: something God planted in a people's culture to prepare them for His message. ([Steve Richardson](https://compelledpodcast.com/episodes/steve-richardson)) Yeshua is the true Peace Child, given by the Father. He never dies, so Peace with God is eternal to those who accept Heaven's Peace Child.

In *Eternity in Their Hearts* (1981), Richardson showed how belief in one supreme God has lasted for centuries in hundreds of cultures. ([Mission Frontiers](https://www.missionfrontiers.org/issue/article/remembering-don-richardson)) One of his clearest cases is the Karen, whose elders kept a story of a lost book from the true God, Y'wa. The Karen are part of LTI's own symposium research.

## Where LTI stands

Gregory changed the decor. Richardson found the doorway. We believe some doorways were seeded by ancient Israel and will be the object of many prophecies in scripture.

We do not claim every people descends from the lost tribes. Israel's scattering works like a culture in milk: a small seed that turns the whole into yogurt. Likewise, the nations became like Israelites when Israel was sifted among them. Scripture's Manasseh son of Jacob became Manas son of Jakyb, the hero of the Kyrgyz people. Not every Kyrgyz comes from Manasseh, but the nation was shaped by the Torah through his descendants, their traditions, and the stories they told. We see the same influence among the Karbi, the Karen, the Mizo and the Japanese.

Those seeds often point to the Anointed One. Messianic Jews showed that a people can follow Yeshua without leaving who they are. The same template holds: to the Jews first, then the nations. Like Messianic Jews, each people can gather to the ancient God of their forefathers in their own language, story and form, without being packaged into someone else's religion and form.

"From one man he made all the nations, that they should inhabit the whole earth; and he marked out their appointed times in history and the boundaries of their lands. God did this so that they would seek him and perhaps reach out for him and find him, though he is not far from any one of us" (Acts 17:26-27). I welcome discussion.
